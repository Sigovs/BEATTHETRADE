A · The line to beat: shows the competition

Idea

The first screen shows the product itself: one auction on one scale, bids crossing the trade-in and your minimum. The process is explained after you've seen it work.

Advantage

Explains the business in two seconds. No competitor (Motorway, Carwow, Carvana, Peddle) shows bids or a line.

Risk

Example numbers can read as a promise, so the "Example" label and caption are load-bearing. The board and the form compete for first look at 1024px and below.

Motion

Bars grow once, oldest bid first (0.9s each). Under reduced motion they're static at their final values.

B · You start it: the visitor takes part

Idea

The hero is the form itself: one plate field at headline scale. Below it, your own trade-in number becomes the line, and you place your minimum on it.

Advantage

The biggest first commitment, and it owns "beat your number". Competitors only quote averages (£1,600 / £1,000 over part-ex).

Risk

Needs the plate and offer passed into /listings/new. The tool must never forecast a result. A plate-styled field can turn cute if it imitates a real MA plate.

Motion

Only feedback: the pins move with your input (320ms). Nothing plays on its own.

    C · One afternoon: shows the auction as time

    Idea

    Scroll is the auction clock. One pinned run from 1:00 to 5:00, where each stage adds a fact: open, first bid, minimum crossed, top bid, close.

    Advantage

    Owns the time-boxed event, which nobody in the category shows, and flips the instant-offer clock: the dealers are on the clock, not you.

    Risk

    Proof arrives after 3+ screens of scroll. The pin costs conversion on mobile, so mobile and reduced motion get a static timeline instead. It's the heaviest build.

    Motion

    Scroll-linked (MAIN for this section only). The clock in the hero shows the real schedule (ET).
