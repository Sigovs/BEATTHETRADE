## Closest analogs (dealers bid)

**Motorway (UK)** — "Sell my car." / "Get £1,600 more than part exchange on average*…"
· input: registration · CTA "Value your car".
Mechanic as static steps: "Thousands of verified dealers bid… We send the highest offer." "That
competition drives your price up." Proof: 1M+ users, 8,000+ dealers, Trustpilot 4.4 (113k), press,
a feed of real sales ("Sold by Derek, BMW X1 for £11,346"). The seller pays a fee. The reserve is barely mentioned.

**Carwow sell (UK)** — "Sell my car" / "…average £1,000 more vs part-exchange quotes*" · input: reg.
"Set your reserve price. We put your car in front of 6,000+ dealers. Best offer wins!" The reserve is
step-2 small print, and a rep phones to "help you agree" it. A comparison table vs Motorway / WeBuyAnyCar
leads on "Completely free to sell". Trustpilot 4.3 (85.9k).

## What it means for BTT

**Table stakes:** plate + state (or VIN) in the hero · one CTA verb · "no obligation" stated · real results feed.

**Unowned, so BTT should own it:**
1. **A personal benchmark.** Everyone compares against part-ex *on average*. BTT asks for *your* actual
   offer, so the page can say "beat your number".
2. **The reserve as the seller's power.** Others bury it or set it with a rep. "You set the line" is unclaimed.
3. **A real, time-boxed event.** Others say "daily auction" vaguely. BTT has a fixed 1–5pm weekday window,
   and nobody shows bids or a clock.
4. **Who pays.** "Dealers pay only if they win" beats Carwow's "free" and Motorway's fee.
5. **Spam and calls.** Nobody names the fear. Answer it (pending the client fact on who sees contacts).
6. **Whose clock.** Instant-offer players put the seller on a clock ("valid 7 days"). BTT puts the *dealers* on a clock.

**Saturated, so not headline material:** "Sell my car" (fine as an identifying eyebrow) · "instant offer" ·
"fast, fair, no fuss" · "no haggle" · "best offer wins" · "highest offer" (also banned for BTT) ·
"same-day payment" · "in 2 minutes / 30 seconds" · "free pickup".
